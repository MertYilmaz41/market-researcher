      Cache

      Downloaded Steam data is kept here so searches run faster. Saved runs and the shortlist are kept. After clearing all, the first search of each tag is slow again.

      How it works

      The method

      Look for games that many people bought (many reviews) but rate poorly. Their players want this kind of game, and the game lets them down. A better game for the same players is the opportunity.

      Searching

      Click the search box at the top and tick one or more Steam tags (games must have all of them), then press Search. To look at a single game, paste its AppID or store link into the same box. Your past searches are listed when the box is empty.

      Filters

      The filter button under the title shows the current filters; click it to change them. Minimum reviews sets how proven the demand must be. The % positive range picks unhappy audiences (5-70% by default). Games to analyze and reviews read per game trade speed for detail. Presets fill in the fields.

      The score

      0 to 100, from two parts: demand (how many reviews) and dissatisfaction (how far below 70% positive). Risks such as an abandoned or fading game subtract points. Hover over any score to see how it was reached. The words beside it ("Many buyers, most unhappy") say what the score rests on. Neither was shown to predict how a new game would do, so use them to choose which games to read about.

      The map

      Further right means more demand, higher up means unhappier players, a bigger bubble means more estimated revenue, and the color is the score. The best opportunities sit at the top right.

      Markets and the unmet share

      The Markets view and the sentences under a search's title describe a whole tag. In a backtest of 180 tags (2019-2022), new games did better where earlier games had done well: in tags whose games of 2-4 years before became hits more often (the track record measured after each search), with many well-served games, and in big markets. The Markets view is ranked by those. The unmet share shows that players keep buying though few games please them; it was not shown to predict success (new games there reached 500 reviews more often, 21% vs 14%, but not 500 reviews at 80%+ positive, 11% vs 12%).

      Complaints

      Complaints are found with keywords in the negative reviews. Checked against hand-labeled reviews, about 80% of the complaints it finds are right and it catches about 64% of them, so read the quotes as well.

      Local AI summaries

      If Ollama runs on your computer, the AI summary button under the title lets a local model read the results: what players love, the complaints the games share and what to build. A game's Summary tab does the same for one game. Nothing leaves your computer. It takes from 20 seconds to about a minute and a half.

      Terms

      What the words on the page mean here. The same explanations appear when you hover or tap an ⓘ.

      Risks

      Each risk a game can get. Most take points off the score; a game's History tab lists every check and how the game came out of it.

      Shortlist and past runs

      Star a game to keep it on the Shortlist with a note, and download the list as CSV. Every finished search is saved and listed under Past searches in the search box; running the same search again shows how the scores changed.
