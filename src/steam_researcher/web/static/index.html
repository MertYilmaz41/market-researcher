Steam Researcher

Proven demand + poor execution = high opportunity

How it works

The method

Look for games that many people bought (many reviews) but rate poorly. Their players want this kind of game, and the game lets them down. A better game for the same players is the opportunity.

Filters

Minimum reviews sets how proven the demand must be. The % positive range picks unhappy audiences (5-70% by default). Games to analyze and reviews read per game trade speed for detail. Presets fill in the fields.

The score

0 to 100, from three parts: demand (how many reviews), dissatisfaction (how far below 70% positive) and fixability (how many complaints a new game could fix). Risks such as an abandoned or fading game subtract points. Hover over any score to see how it was reached.

The map

Further right means more demand, higher up means unhappier players, a bigger bubble means more estimated revenue, and the color is the score. The best opportunities sit at the top right. The 3D view adds fixability as depth.

Complaints

Complaints are found with keywords in the negative reviews. Checked against hand-labeled reviews, about 80% of the complaints it finds are right and it catches about 64% of them, so read the quotes as well.

Local AI summaries

If Ollama runs on your computer, Write summary lets a local model read the results: what players love, the complaints the games share and what to build. Nothing leaves your computer. It takes from 20 seconds to about a minute and a half.

Terms

What the words on the page mean here. The same explanations appear when you hover or tap an ⓘ.

Risks

Each risk a game can get. Most take points off the score; the game's Risks tab says why it got one.

Shortlist and past runs

Star a game to keep it on the Shortlist with a note, and download the list as CSV. Every finished search is saved under Past runs, and running the same search again shows how the scores changed.
